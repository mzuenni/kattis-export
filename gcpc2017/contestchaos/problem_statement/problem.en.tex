\problemname{Contest Chaos}

This is a story about Gregor.
Gregor is studying computer science at a very famous university.
When his friends told him about the upcoming \emph{GCPC}, he immediately knew he wanted to take part.
Moreover, he would be the very best - like no one ever was - and win the whole contest.

To win the GCPC, he would train himself extra hard and solve ALL the practice problems.
He immediately started to plan his contest domination and looked up which practice contests were available.
He even assigned them a difficulty rating, since of course harder problems meant more and better training. 

Of course he wants to take part in all the contests, but unfortunately some of them overlap, so he has to decide which ones to take part in.
Can you help him plan his schedule and win the GCPC?

\section*{Input}
The input starts with a line containing $n (1 \leq n \leq 5000)$, the amount of practice contests that take place before the GCPC.
$n$ lines follow, one for each contest. 
To get rid of timezone issues, Gregor already converted all the start and end times of the contests to ``hours from now''.
Each line consists of $3$ numbers and a string, $a$ $b$ $p$ $s$ $(1 \leq a \leq b \leq 5000, 1 \leq p \leq 10000)$, where the contest named $s$ ($s$ is of length between $1$ and $30$ characters and contains only characters ``a'' to ``z'' or ``A'' to ``Z'') starts $a$ hours from now, ends $b$ hours from now and has a difficulty rating of $p$.
Two contests overlap even if the first one ends during the same hour the second one starts.

\section*{Output}
Output one line containing the maximum combined difficulty rating of all the practice contests he can do without taking part in overlapping contests.
